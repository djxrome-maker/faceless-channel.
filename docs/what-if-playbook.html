What If… For 5 Seconds

Our niche is animated science "what if" Shorts. Each one asks an impossible question, walks through the chain of things that would happen, flips it with a twist, and ends on a YES/NO question that loops back to the start.

Hook: ask the impossible question

Say the title out loud while the title text sits at the top of the screen. The first frame already shows the impossible thing happening.

"If friction disappeared for 5 seconds…"

0:03–0:08

Setup: the fun part people expect

Show one shot of the cool fantasy version, so the viewer leans in.

"At first, it'd feel like ice skating everywhere."

0:08–0:30

Consequence chain: 5–6 quick results

Show one consequence per shot, about 3 seconds each. Give each a yellow stat badge, like "SPEED: 1,000 MPH". Every claim gets fact-checked.

"Your shoes slide off. Knots untie. Every car on Earth keeps rolling…"

0:30–0:40

Twist: "But here's the real problem…"

Reveal the counter-intuitive science that ruins the fantasy. This is what makes people rewatch and argue in the comments.

0:40–0:50

Payoff and the YES/NO question

End on a short ironic punchline, then cut back to the opening shot and ask "Would you risk it? YES or NO." The video loops straight back into the hook.

Exact specs

What I'll build every time

Length
45–55 seconds. The top videos are 45–60.
Script
115–130 words, read at about 150 words a minute. Short, punchy sentences.
Cuts
12–14 shots, each about 3–3.5 seconds. Every shot has a slow push-in or pan, so nothing sits still.
Voice
A calm, curious male narrator with a documentary feel, pausing between beats.
Captions
Bold white capitals with a black outline, 1–3 words at a time, timed to each word. The spoken word turns yellow. They sit in the lower middle of the screen.
Top text
The title question stays at the top for the first 3 seconds, with the key word in yellow.
Visuals
Stylized 3D cartoon, like a glossy animated movie, with the same character in every episode.
Sound
A low synth bed, a whoosh on every cut, a bass hit on every stat badge, and a sudden silence at the "freeze" moment.
Ending
Back to the first shot, then the YES/NO question.

What makes it ours

We copy the shape, not their videos

One recurring character

The top channel uses a generic guy. Ours will be a single named mascot, so viewers recognize us in two frames. It also protects us from YouTube's "repetitive content" rule.

Our own topics

We won't remake their titles. We use the same "For 5 Seconds" pattern on questions they haven't covered.

Real, sourced science

Perplexity checks every number before we animate it. Their comments are full of people correcting the physics, and we want to be the channel that gets it right.

Original everything

The visuals are AI-made, the music is original or royalty-free, and the voice is AI. We never use anyone else's footage or songs.
